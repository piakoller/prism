Clinical Assessment:
**Patient Clinical Summary**
The patient is a 39-year-old female diagnosed with a well-differentiated Ileal Neuroendocrine Tumor (Si-NET). The disease is classified as G2, though there is a discrepancy in the reported proliferation index between the 2023 biopsy (Ki-67 6%) and the surgical description (Ki-67 2%). Staging indicates pT2 pN1 pM1, with metastases confirmed in the liver and peritoneum.

**Clinical Course and Treatment Response**
The patient presented with progressive disease (PD) in 2024 involving liver metastases and the primary tumor while on long-acting Somatostatin Analogues (SSA). Following a multidisciplinary discussion, she underwent 4 cycles of PRRT (June 2024–November 2024). Re-evaluation in February 2025 demonstrated a partial response (PR) in both the liver metastases and the primary tumor. The patient remains in good clinical condition.

**Guideline-Based Assessment**

**1. Systemic Therapy (PRRT) Assessment**
*   **Indication:** The initiation of PRRT following progression on first-line SSA aligns with the *ENETS 2024 guidance paper*, which states that PRRT is the recommended second-line treatment in the presence of SST-positive Si-NET (Recommendation A-1b).
*   **Response:** The observed partial response validates the decision to utilize PRRT.
*   **Eligibility Note:** It is acknowledged that PRRT eligibility generally requires DOTATATE/DOTATOC PET uptake higher than liver background in all primary tumor lesions and metastases. The patient's Ga-68-DOTATOC PET/CT confirmed uptake in liver lesions and the ileum.

**2. Surgical Assessment (Upfront vs. Delayed/Combined)**
*   **Primary Tumor Resection:** The *ENETS 2024 guidelines* note that the role of prophylactic primary tumor resection in asymptomatic stage IV patients is controversial. While some retrospective data suggest a survival benefit, propensity-score matched studies show no benefit compared to no resection. Crucially, the guidelines cite evidence that delayed locoregional surgery (>6 months from diagnosis) is associated with similar overall survival compared to upfront surgery.
*   **Combination Strategy:** The guidelines highlight recent retrospective studies indicating that the combination of Si-NET resection and PRRT provides the best disease-specific survival for patients with diffuse stage IV disease. The patient's course (PRRT followed by proposed surgery) aligns with this multimodal approach.
*   **Peritoneal Metastases:** The patient has peritoneal involvement (pM1). *ENETS 2024* (Recommendation A-4) states that cytoreductive peritoneal surgery is recommended in patients with localized peritoneal carcinomatosis.
*   **Liver Metastases:** For Stage IV disease with exclusive liver metastases, surgery is indicated if R0 resection is achievable (Recommendation B-4). In non-surgical candidates or those with diffuse disease, liver-directed therapies or systemic treatments (like the PRRT already administered) are indicated.

**Conclusion regarding the Tumor Board Question**
Regarding the sequencing of therapy ("PRRT directly" vs. "surgery upfront"):
*   The decision to proceed with PRRT directly upon progression in 2024 was consistent with guidelines for SSA-refractory disease.
*   The current proposal for surgery (post-PRRT) is supported by guideline evidence suggesting that delayed surgery or a combination of PRRT and resection may offer survival benefits.
*   Upfront surgery in the presence of extra-abdominal metastases (if any were present) or extensive peritoneal disease is generally discouraged by *ESMO 2020 guidelines*, which note that upfront surgery should be excluded in the presence of extra-abdominal metastases.
*   Given the patient's young age (39) and partial response to PRRT, a surgical approach now to address the primary tumor (pT2) and peritoneal metastases (pM1) is a valid consideration within the scope of cytoreductive strategies supported by ENETS.

	•	Summary: This research study is a Phase II clinical trial, which tests the safety and effectiveness of an investigational drug to learn whether the drug works in treating a specific cancer. "Investigational" means that the drug, Ziv-aflibercept, is being studied. It also means that the FDA has not yet approved Ziv-aflibercept for use in patients with your type of cancer.

Every person has molecules in their bloodstream called vascular endothelial growth factors (VEGFs). These molecules help grow and sustain new blood vessels needed by the human body. Cancer tumors hijack this mechanism because they need new blod vessels and oxygen to grow. Ziv-aflibercept is an antibody. Antibodies are proteins that are produced naturally in our bodies and help to recognize foreign substances in our body. Ziv-aflibercept is a "targeted therapy" called a "VEGF Trap", that "traps" (binds) these VEGFs and prevents the cancer from using them to grow.

Though Ziv-aflibercept has not yet been FDA approved for the treatment of carcinoid tumors, it has recently been approved for patients with treatment-resistant colorectal cancer.

In this research study, we will use Ziv-aflibercept in combination with standard octreotide therapy to see if it slows the growth or spread of your carcinoid tumor. Standard octreotide (sandostatin) therapy is currently approved for treating symptoms of carcinoid tumors, such as those caused by carcinoid syndrome. Carcinoid syndrome is caused by hormones and other substances released by carcinoid tumors into the bloodstream. One of these secreted substances is serotonin, one of the body's natural chemical messengers. When excess serotonin secreted by the carcinoid tumors reaches the body's tissues, it is thought to cause diarrhea and redness (flushing) of the face, chest or back. Excess serotonin may also cause changes in the structure of the heart valves, which can impair the heart's function. Octreotide works by binding to receptors found on carcinoid tumors and prevents the release of hormones from the tumor.

This trial investigated the activity of Ziv-aflibercept, a recombinant protein that binds to and inhibits the activity of VEGF, in patients with advanced NETs ( NCT01782443 ). Methods: A single-arm, phase II trial enrolling patients with advanced, progressive extrapancreatic NET. Patients were treated with Ziv-aflibercept 4 mg/kg intravenously on day 1 and 15 of a 28-day cycle; the starting dose was reduced to 2 mg/kg on days 1 and 15 of a 28-day cycle because of hypertension-related events. The primary end point was progression-free survival. Results: The trial enrolled 19 patients (13 male:6 female). Patients received a median of 7 cycles (range, 1-18 cycles). The median progression free survival was 11.8 months (95% confidence interval, 3.2-16.1 months), and the median overall survival was 36.4 months (95% confidence interval, 16.1-not reached). Best responses by Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors 1.1 are as follows: 1 (5%) partial response, 13 (68%) stable disease, 2 (10%) with progressive disease, and 3 (15%) unevaluable.
