**Guideline-Based Assessment**

**1. Systemic Therapy (PRRT) Assessment**
*   **Indication:** The initiation of PRRT following progression on first-line SSA aligns with the *ENETS 2024 guidance paper*, which states that PRRT is the recommended second-line treatment in the presence of SST-positive Si-NET (Recommendation A-1b).
*   **Response:** The observed partial response validates the decision to utilize PRRT.
*   **Eligibility Note:** It is acknowledged that PRRT eligibility generally requires DOTATATE/DOTATOC PET uptake higher than liver background in all primary tumor lesions and metastases. The patient's Ga-68-DOTATOC PET/CT confirmed uptake in liver lesions and the ileum.

**2. Surgical Assessment (Upfront vs. Delayed/Combined)**
*   **Primary Tumor Resection:** The *ENETS 2024 guidelines* note that the role of prophylactic primary tumor resection in asymptomatic stage IV patients is controversial. While some retrospective data suggest a survival benefit, propensity-score matched studies show no benefit compared to no resection. Crucially, the guidelines cite evidence that delayed locoregional surgery (>6 months from diagnosis) is associated with similar overall survival compared to upfront surgery.
*   **Combination Strategy:** The guidelines highlight recent retrospective studies indicating that the combination of Si-NET resection and PRRT provides the best disease-specific survival for patients with diffuse stage IV disease. The patient's course (PRRT followed by proposed surgery) aligns with this multimodal approach.
*   **Peritoneal Metastases:** The patient has peritoneal involvement (pM1). *ENETS 2024* (Recommendation A-4) states that cytoreductive peritoneal surgery is recommended in patients with localized peritoneal carcinomatosis.
*   **Liver Metastases:** For Stage IV disease with exclusive liver metastases, surgery is indicated if R0 resection is achievable (Recommendation B-4). In non-surgical candidates or those with diffuse disease, liver-directed therapies or systemic treatments (like the PRRT already administered) are indicated.

**Conclusion regarding the Tumor Board Question**

*   The decision to proceed with PRRT directly upon progression in 2024 was consistent with guidelines for SSA-refractory disease.
*   The current proposal for surgery (post-PRRT) is supported by guideline evidence suggesting that delayed surgery or a combination of PRRT and resection may offer survival benefits.
