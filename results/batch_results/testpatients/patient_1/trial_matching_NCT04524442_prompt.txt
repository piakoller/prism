### **Diagnosis and Staging**
- **Histopathology:** The current diagnosis is a well-differentiated neuroendocrine tumor (NET) Grade 2, with a Ki-67 of 19%. According to both the ENETS 2023 and ESMO 2020 guidelines, a Ki-67 between 3% and 20% defines a G2 tumor. - **Staging:** Imaging (CT scans from April and May 2025) demonstrates a large, 10 cm mass in the pancreatic tail. ### **Tumor Characteristics and Prognostic Factors**
- **FDG Avidity:** The initial FDG-PET scan revealed a highly hypermetabolic mass with an SUV of 16. The ENETS 2023 guidelines note that high FDG uptake correlates with a higher grade and is a strong poor prognostic factor. The ESMO 2020 guidelines concur that FDG-positive NETs indicate a worse prognosis. This high FDG avidity, combined with the high-end G2 Ki-67 index, underscores the aggressive nature of this tumor.

### **Clinical Course and Response to Prior Therapy**
- **Clinical Presentation:** The patient's clinical course has been dominated by severe, recurrent upper GI bleeding, requiring ICU admission, arterial embolization, and blood transfusions (recent Hb 7.1 g/dL). - **Effect of Chemotherapy:** The patient received 7 cycles of FOLFIRINOX. While not a standard regimen for NETs, the tumor size did show a modest decrease from 12 cm to 10 cm. The ENETS and ESMO guidelines discuss the use of neoadjuvant or conversion therapy for locally advanced tumors to potentially improve resectability. Although unintentional, the FOLFIRINOX treatment can be viewed in this context, having achieved at least disease stabilization in a bulky, aggressive tumor.
