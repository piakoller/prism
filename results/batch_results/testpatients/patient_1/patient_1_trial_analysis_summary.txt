TRIALS TO INCLUDE IN RECOMMENDATION:
  ✅ NCT02730104: Community-based Neuroendocrine Tumor (NET) Research Study
     Rationale: This observational study provides real-world evidence supporting the efficacy and tolerability of lanreotide, a standard-of-care somatostatin analog (SSA). This is relevant as SSAs are a foundational therapy for controlling tumor growth in patients with SSTR-positive NETs.
  ✅ NCT03049189: Efficacy and Safety of 177Lu-edotreotide PRRT in GEP-NET Patients
     Rationale: The COMPETE trial demonstrated a significant progression-free survival (PFS) benefit for PRRT (177Lu-edotreotide) over everolimus in G1/G2 GEP-NETs. This provides strong evidence for considering PRRT as a primary therapeutic option for this patient, pending confirmation of SSTR-positivity.
  ✅ NCT05153772: Targeted Alpha-emitter Therapy of PRRT Naïve and Previous PRRT Neuroendocrine Tumor Patients
     Rationale: This trial of an alpha-emitter PRRT (212Pb-DOTAMTATE) showed a very high objective response rate (56.8%) in PRRT-naïve patients. This suggests a potentially more potent form of PRRT that could be highly effective for downstaging this patient's large, locally advanced tumor.
  ✅ NCT02236910: An Open Label Registry Study of Lutetium-177 (DOTA0, TYR3) Octreotate (Lu-DOTA-TATE) Treatment in Patients with Somatostatin Receptor Positive Tumours
     Rationale: This study provides further real-world evidence on the efficacy of standard PRRT (177Lu-dotatate), reporting a median PFS of 34.1 months. It supports the use of PRRT and helps in understanding prognostic factors relevant to this patient's high-burden disease.
  ✅ NCT03074513: Atezolizumab and Bevacizumab in Treating Patients With Rare Solid Tumors
     Rationale: This trial demonstrated that the combination of atezolizumab and bevacizumab has clinical activity in pancreatic NETs, with a 20% response rate and a 14.9-month median PFS. This represents a viable systemic therapy option for this patient, especially given the tumor's aggressive and FDG-avid nature.
  ✅ NCT03873870: 68Ga-DOTATATE PET for Management of Neuroendocrine Tumors
     Rationale: This is a diagnostic study, not a therapeutic one, but it is critically important as it validates the use of 68Ga-DOTATATE PET/CT for confirming SSTR expression. This imaging is the essential next step for the patient to determine eligibility for SSTR-targeted therapies like PRRT and SSAs.
  ✅ NCT02754297: Personalized PRRT of Neuroendocrine Tumors
     Rationale: This trial demonstrates that a personalized, dosimetry-guided approach to PRRT can safely increase the radiation dose delivered to the tumor and shows promising response rates in pNETs. This supports using an optimized PRRT strategy to maximize the chance of response for this patient's large tumor.
  ✅ NCT03728361: Nivolumab and Temozolomide in Treating Patients With Recurrent or Refractory Small-Cell Lung Cancer or Advanced Neuroendocrine Cancer
     Rationale: This trial showed promising activity for the combination of nivolumab and temozolomide in NENs, with a 32.1% overall response rate. This chemo-immunotherapy combination is a relevant systemic treatment option for this patient's aggressive, high-end G2 tumor.
  ✅ NCT03217097: MGMT-NET: O6-methylguanine-DNA Methyltransferase (MGMT) Status in Neuroendocrine Tumors: Predictive Factor of Response to Alkylating Agents
     Rationale: This study provides evidence that MGMT testing can predict response to alkylating agent chemotherapy (like temozolomide). This is highly relevant for guiding the choice of chemotherapy for this patient, helping to decide between an alkylating agent or an oxaliplatin-based regimen.
  ✅ NCT04552847: Al18F-NOTA-octreotide PET Imaging in Neuroendocrine Tumors
     Rationale: This diagnostic study shows that 18F-AlF-NOTA-octreotide PET is superior to standard 68Ga-DOTA-SSA PET for lesion detection. It validates the critical need for SSTR-PET imaging and presents a potentially more sensitive imaging option to fully stage the patient and confirm SSTR expression.
  ✅ NCT05448157: 68Ga-DOTATOC Radio-Guided Surgery With β-Probe in GEP-NET
     Rationale: This trial demonstrates a highly accurate intraoperative technique (radio-guided surgery) to detect NET lesions. As definitive local control via surgery is a primary goal for this patient, this evidence is relevant for planning a procedure that maximizes the chance of complete resection.
  ✅ NCT01842165: 177Lutetium-octreotate Treatment Prediction Using Multimodality Imaging in Refractory NETs
     Rationale: This study shows that tumor-absorbed dose and early changes on SSTR-PET imaging during PRRT can predict long-term outcomes. This information is valuable for managing and monitoring PRRT if it is chosen as a therapy for this patient.
  ✅ NCT03375320: Testing Cabozantinib in Patients With Advanced Pancreatic Neuroendocrine and Carcinoid Tumors
     Rationale: The phase 3 CABINET trial established a significant PFS benefit for cabozantinib in patients with progressive pancreatic NETs. This makes cabozantinib a strong evidence-based systemic therapy option for this patient, likely after other lines of therapy or if local control is not achievable.
  ✅ NCT03972488: Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Lutathera in Patients With Grade 2 and Grade 3 Advanced GEP-NET
     Rationale: The NETTER-2 trial is highly relevant, as it demonstrated a profound PFS benefit for first-line 177Lu-DOTA-TATE in patients with high-risk G2/G3 GEP-NETs, a profile that perfectly matches this patient. This trial establishes PRRT as a standard-of-care consideration for this patient.

TRIALS TO EXCLUDE FROM RECOMMENDATION:
  ❌ NCT03278379: Avelumab in G2-3 NET
     Rationale: The published results for this trial of single-agent PD-L1 blockade showed "limited antitumour activity" and no objective responses in patients with G2-3 NETs, making it an unpromising option for this patient.
  ❌ NCT02568267: Basket Study of Entrectinib (RXDX-101) for the Treatment of Patients With Solid Tumors Harboring NTRK 1/2/3 (Trk A/B/C), ROS1, or ALK Gene Rearrangements (Fusions)
     Rationale: This trial is for a targeted therapy (entrectinib) that is only effective in tumors with rare NTRK, ROS1, or ALK fusions. As the patient's tumor has not been tested for these specific alterations, this therapy is not currently indicated and would only be a consideration if such a fusion were identified.
  ❌ NCT02834013: Nivolumab and Ipilimumab in Treating Patients With Rare Tumors
     Rationale: The DART trial showed that dual checkpoint blockade (nivolumab + ipilimumab) was effective in high-grade neuroendocrine carcinomas but had a 0% response rate in low/intermediate-grade tumors. As this patient has a well-differentiated G2 tumor, this therapy is not supported by the evidence.
  ❌ NCT03070301: A Study of LEE011 With Everolimus in Patients With Advanced Neuroendocrine Tumors
     Rationale: This trial of ribociclib plus everolimus was terminated due to insufficient activity and toxicity. The combination showed no objective responses and should not be considered for this patient.
  ❌ NCT01253161: Study of Pasireotide Long Acting Release (LAR) in Patients With Metastatic Neuroendocrine Tumors (NETs)
     Rationale: While pasireotide showed some anti-proliferative effect, it was associated with a very high rate of hyperglycemia (79%). Given the availability of other effective SSAs and therapies with better safety profiles, the toxicity of pasireotide makes it a less favorable option.
  ❌ NCT01563354: 3-arm Trial to Evaluate Pasireotide LAR/Everolimus Alone/in Combination in Patients With Lung/Thymus NET - LUNA Trial
     Rationale: This trial's patient population was limited to lung and thymus NETs. As the patient has a pancreatic NET, the results are not directly applicable, and other trials specifically addressing pNETs provide more relevant evidence.
  ❌ NCT02893930: Sapanisertib in Treating Patients With Metastatic or Refractory Pancreatic Neuroendocrine Tumor That Cannot Be Removed by Surgery
     Rationale: This phase II trial of sapanisertib, an mTORC1/2 inhibitor, was terminated early due to a lack of objective responses in patients with rapalog-resistant pNETs. The drug showed no meaningful clinical activity and should not be recommended.
