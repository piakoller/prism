DECISION RULE: When does a study provide meaningful evidence for therapy recommendations?

Answer YES if ALL THREE criteria are met; otherwise answer NO.

	1.	**Disease / Biology Alignment** (should align reasonably)
Core disease features should be reasonably concordant: differentiation (NET vs NEC), primary site/subtype, grade/Ki-67 range, stage (localized vs advanced/metastatic), functional status. The study population should be clinically similar enough that treatment insights are transferable.

	•	"Reasonably concordant" allows clinically justified extrapolation (e.g., pancreatic NET G2 evidence may inform pancreatic NET G1-G2 treatment decisions, midgut NET evidence may apply to small bowel NET with similar characteristics).

	2.	**Treatment Context / Clinical Applicability** (should be applicable in at least one clinical scenario)
The study's treatment approach, setting, and patient population should provide meaningful evidence for at least one relevant clinical scenario for this patient. Consider:
	•	**Current state:** Patient's actual clinical situation
	•	**Alternative scenarios:** e.g. What if surgery is/isn't feasible? What if SSTR-PET is positive? Different treatment sequences?
	•	**Treatment approach:** Is the studied intervention something that could be considered for this patient?
	•	**Eligibility context:** While strict eligibility criteria shouldn't be a hard rejection, consider if the study population is reasonably similar to this patient.
	•	**Evidence transferability:** Can the treatment insights from this study population be reasonably applied to this patient's situation?

	3.	**Evidence Quality and Relevance** (should provide actionable clinical insights)
The published results should offer meaningful evidence that could guide treatment decisions:
	•	Study provides clear efficacy and/or safety data
	•	Results are clinically meaningful (not just statistical significance)
	•	Patient population studied is representative enough to inform decisions for this patient
	•	**Eligibility considerations:** While the patient may not meet all inclusion criteria, the study population should be sufficiently similar that treatment insights are transferable
	•	Evidence strength justifies consideration in treatment planning

**IMPORTANT:** Publication quality is not a standalone criterion. Focus on whether the prospective clinical evidence (even from preliminary results or abstracts) provides meaningful insights for treatment decisions.

If any of the three criteria cannot be verified as met from the provided fields AND reasonable clinical assumptions, respond NO.
Apply clinical judgment when evaluating borderline cases - prioritize studies with strong biological rationale, meaningful clinical outcomes, and evidence that could realistically inform treatment decisions for this patient.

### **Patient Summary**
This is a 52-year-old man, initially presenting with life-threatening upper gastrointestinal (GI) bleeding, who was found to have a large pancreatic mass. After an initial course of FOLFIRINOX chemotherapy under a presumed diagnosis of pancreatic adenocarcinoma, his tumor has been reclassified on pathology review as a nonfunctioning pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor (NF-Pan-NET), Grade 2. He now presents with a new episode of significant GI bleeding and is referred for further management of this locally advanced, symptomatic tumor.

### **Diagnosis and Staging**
- **Histopathology:** The current diagnosis is a well-differentiated neuroendocrine tumor (NET) Grade 2, with a Ki-67 of 19%. According to both the ENETS 2023 and ESMO 2020 guidelines, a Ki-67 between 3% and 20% defines a G2 tumor. This patient's proliferation index is at the highest end of the G2 spectrum, suggesting a more aggressive biological potential.
- **Staging:** Imaging (CT scans from April and May 2025) demonstrates a large, 10 cm mass in the pancreatic tail. The tumor is locally advanced, consistent with a T4 stage, showing infiltration into the splenic hilum, gastric fundus, and left adrenal gland. There is also evidence of complete splenic vein occlusion with a tumor thrombus. The initial FDG-PET scan from January 2025 did not show evidence of distant metastatic disease.

### **Tumor Characteristics and Prognostic Factors**
- **FDG Avidity:** The initial FDG-PET scan revealed a highly hypermetabolic mass with an SUV of 16. The ENETS 2023 guidelines note that high FDG uptake correlates with a higher grade and is a strong poor prognostic factor. The ESMO 2020 guidelines concur that FDG-positive NETs indicate a worse prognosis. This high FDG avidity, combined with the high-end G2 Ki-67 index, underscores the aggressive nature of this tumor.
- **Functional Imaging:** The patient has not had somatostatin receptor (SSTR)-based imaging (e.g., 68Ga-DOTATATE PET/CT). Both the ENETS and ESMO guidelines recommend SSTR-PET/CT as the functional imaging modality of choice for staging, characterization, and theranostic evaluation of NF-Pan-NETs. The absence of this information is a limitation in the current workup, particularly for evaluating SSTR expression which could inform future therapeutic options.

### **Clinical Course and Response to Prior Therapy**
- **Clinical Presentation:** The patient's clinical course has been dominated by severe, recurrent upper GI bleeding, requiring ICU admission, arterial embolization, and blood transfusions (recent Hb 7.1 g/dL). This highlights that the tumor is not only large but also highly symptomatic and locally destructive.
- **Effect of Chemotherapy:** The patient received 7 cycles of FOLFIRINOX. While not a standard regimen for NETs, the tumor size did show a modest decrease from 12 cm to 10 cm. The ENETS and ESMO guidelines discuss the use of neoadjuvant or conversion therapy for locally advanced tumors to potentially improve resectability. Although unintentional, the FOLFIRINOX treatment can be viewed in this context, having achieved at least disease stabilization in a bulky, aggressive tumor.

### **Key Considerations for Therapeutic Planning**
- The central clinical challenge is the management of a large, symptomatic, locally advanced but non-metastatic NF-Pan-NET with high-risk features.
- Despite the tumor's aggressive characteristics (large size, high Ki-67, high FDG uptake, vascular invasion), the disease appears to be confined to the locoregional area based on available imaging.
- The ENETS 2023 guidelines state that patients with locally advanced NF-Pan-NET (stage T4) can be safely resected in expert centers and that radical (R0) resection, including venous resection, could be considered (Recommendations 3.1.4.1 and 3.1.4.2). The ESMO 2020 guidelines similarly support considering pancreatectomy with vascular resection for locally advanced disease.
- The patient's history of recurrent, life-threatening hemorrhage from tumor infiltration makes achieving definitive local control a high priority after clinical stabilization.

	•	Summary: The purpose of this study is to learn if a new drug, ONC201 can make tumors become smaller or go away completely. Investigators also want to learn if ONC201 can prevent new deposits of cancer from appearing in new places in participants (metastases). A phase 2 study of ONC201 in PC-PG (pheochromocytoma-paraganglioma) and other neuroendocrine tumors will determine whether inhibition of DRD2 (a member of the dopamine receptor family) is safe in unresectable, recurrent, locally advanced, refractory, or metastatic neuroendocrine cancers including PC-PG, desmoplastic small round cell tumor (DSRCT), Ewing sarcoma (PNET) or any other neuroendicrine tumor with a catecholamine or dopamine biomarker or autocrine or paracrine dependence on dopamine including cholangiocarcinoma and adrenal cortical carcinoma.

ONC201 is an investigational (experimental) agent and has a favorable safety profile in phase 1 and early phase 2 clinical trials in advanced cancers. This study design has been chosen to see whether ONC201 is associated with reduction of anti-hypertension medications, safety and significant efficacy against neuroendocrine tumors, especially PC-PG.

ONC201 is a bitopic DRD2 antagonist with preclinical ONC201 activity in desmoplastic small round cell tumor (DSRCT). Patients and methods: Patients (N = 30) with neuroendocrine tumors were treated on this investigator-initiated trial ( NCT03034200 ). ONC201 dose and schedule were 625 mg orally weekly in cohorts A (PC-PG) + B (other neuroendocrine tumors) and 625 mg orally on 2 consecutive days each week in cohort C, which included 5 responding patients. The primary endpoint was radiographic response measured using RECIST. Secondary endpoints included progression-free survival, overall survival, and safety. Results: In arm A (n = 10; all PC-PG), 50% (5/10) exhibited a partial response (PR) and 2 additional patients had stable disease (SD) >3 months. Median duration of therapy for arm A patients was 9 months (range: 1.5-33 months) with 5 patients treated >1 year. In arm B (n = 12), there were 1 PR (DSRCT) and 2 SD (DSRCT; neuroblastoma) >3 months. Median duration of therapy in arm A was 18 months (range: 1-33 months) and arm B was 3 months (range: 1.5-33 months). Arm C PC-PG (N = 8) showed 1 PR and 7 SD at 3 months, with median duration of therapy >10 months. There was no decline in Karnofsky performance status at week 12 for 28 of 30 patients and no dose modification due to treatment-related adverse events. Conclusions: Oral ONC201 was well tolerated in patients with metastatic neuroendocrine tumors and associated with clinical benefit, including tumor responses, particularly in some patients with DSRCT and the majority of patients with PC-PG.
