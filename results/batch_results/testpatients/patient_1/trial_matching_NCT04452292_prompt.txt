This is a 52-year-old man, initially presenting with life-threatening upper gastrointestinal (GI) bleeding, who was found to have a large pancreatic mass. ### **Diagnosis and Staging**
- **Histopathology:** The current diagnosis is a well-differentiated neuroendocrine tumor (NET) Grade 2, with a Ki-67 of 19%. According to both the ENETS 2023 and ESMO 2020 guidelines, a Ki-67 between 3% and 20% defines a G2 tumor. This patient's proliferation index is at the highest end of the G2 spectrum, suggesting a more aggressive biological potential.
- **Staging:** Imaging (CT scans from April and May 2025) demonstrates a large, 10 cm mass in the pancreatic tail. The tumor is locally advanced, consistent with a T4 stage, showing infiltration into the splenic hilum, gastric fundus, and left adrenal gland. There is also evidence of complete splenic vein occlusion with a tumor thrombus. The initial FDG-PET scan from January 2025 did not show evidence of distant metastatic disease.

### **Tumor Characteristics and Prognostic Factors**
- **FDG Avidity:** The initial FDG-PET scan revealed a highly hypermetabolic mass with an SUV of 16. The ENETS 2023 guidelines note that high FDG uptake correlates with a higher grade and is a strong poor prognostic factor. The ESMO 2020 guidelines concur that FDG-positive NETs indicate a worse prognosis. This high FDG avidity, combined with the high-end G2 Ki-67 index, underscores the aggressive nature of this tumor.
- **Functional Imaging:** The patient has not had somatostatin receptor (SSTR)-based imaging (e.g., 68Ga-DOTATATE PET/CT). Both the ENETS and ESMO guidelines recommend SSTR-PET/CT as the functional imaging modality of choice for staging, characterization, and theranostic evaluation of NF-Pan-NETs. The absence of this information is a limitation in the current workup, particularly for evaluating SSTR expression which could inform future therapeutic options.

### **Clinical Course and Response to Prior Therapy**
- **Clinical Presentation:** The patient's clinical course has been dominated by severe, recurrent upper GI bleeding, requiring ICU admission, arterial embolization, and blood transfusions (recent Hb 7.1 g/dL). This highlights that the tumor is not only large but also highly symptomatic and locally destructive.
- **Effect of Chemotherapy:** The patient received 7 cycles of FOLFIRINOX. While not a standard regimen for NETs, the tumor size did show a modest decrease from 12 cm to 10 cm. The ENETS and ESMO guidelines discuss the use of neoadjuvant or conversion therapy for locally advanced tumors to potentially improve resectability. Although unintentional, the FOLFIRINOX treatment can be viewed in this context, having achieved at least disease stabilization in a bulky, aggressive tumor.

### **Key Considerations for Therapeutic Planning**
- The central clinical challenge is the management of a large, symptomatic, locally advanced but non-metastatic NF-Pan-NET with high-risk features.
- Despite the tumor's aggressive characteristics (large size, high Ki-67, high FDG uptake, vascular invasion), the disease appears to be confined to the locoregional area based on available imaging.
- The ENETS 2023 guidelines state that patients with locally advanced NF-Pan-NET (stage T4) can be safely resected in expert centers and that radical (R0) resection, including venous resection, could be considered (Recommendations 3.1.4.1 and 3.1.4.2). The ESMO 2020 guidelines similarly support considering pancreatectomy with vascular resection for locally advanced disease.
- The patient's history of recurrent, life-threatening hemorrhage from tumor infiltration makes achieving definitive local control a high priority after clinical stabilization.

PubMed Full Content: Full text links Cite Display options Display options Format Abstract PubMed PMID Abstract Introduction: Large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma (LCNEC) is a rare and highly aggressive high-grade neuroendocrine neoplasm, which can arise from anywhere in the body. Due to its rarity there is a lacuna in our understanding of LCNEC's molecular biology. In 2016, Rekhtman and colleagues presented one of the largest molecular sequencing series of pulmonary LCNEC. They differentiated genomic profiles of LCNEC into two major subsets: small cell lung cancer (SCLC)-like, characterized by TP53 + RB1 co-mutation/loss, and non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC)-like, characterized by the lack of co-altered TP53 + RB1. This finding is of significance because at present LCNEC patients are often treated like SCLC. However, the universal genomic SCLC biomarker of TP53 and RB1 co-mutation was only found in 40% of their cohort. Since then various other scientists have looked into molecular profiling of LCNEC with markedly discordant results. The objective of this study was to conduct a systematic review of publicly available next generation sequencing (NGS) data to evaluate the prevalence of TP53 + RB1 co-mutation in LCNEC. Method: We conducted a literature search using PubMed. Seven studies including 302 patients with pulmonary LCNEC and four studies including 20 patients with extra-pulmonary LCNEC underwent final analysis. Results: The prevalence of TP53 + RB1 co-mutation was 36% (109/302) among pulmonary LCNEC patients and 35% (7/20) among the extra-thoracic LCNEC cohort. This finding is in stark contrast to >90% TP53 + RB1 co-mutation in SCLC. Conclusion: It is now well established that LCNEC is molecularly distinct from SCLC. LCNEC seems to have two molecularly defined sub-cohort based on TP53 + RB1 co-mutation status. Future studies should look into prognostic and predictive implication of TP53 + RB1 co-mutation status in LCNEC. Prospective studies should be designed to characterize molecular subtypes and direct treatment accordingly. We are currently conducting a prospective pilot clinical trial wherein LCNEC patients are treated based on TP53 + RB1 co-mutation status. The study is currently enrolling.
