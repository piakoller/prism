### **Patient Summary**
This is a 52-year-old man with a recently diagnosed, large, locally advanced pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor (Pan-NET). He initially presented with a life-threatening upper gastrointestinal (GI) bleed and was empirically treated with 7 cycles of FOLFIRINOX. A subsequent pathology review reclassified his tumor as a well-differentiated Grade 2 NET with a Ki-67 of 19%. He is now admitted for a second episode of severe upper GI bleeding, with a hemoglobin of 7.1 g/dL, secondary to direct tumor infiltration into the stomach.

### **Tumor Characteristics and Staging**
*   **Histopathology:** The diagnosis is a well-differentiated Pan-NET, Grade 2. The Ki-67 proliferation index of 19% is at the highest end of the G2 category (G2 range: 3-20%), bordering on G3, which often indicates a more aggressive clinical course (ESMO 2020, Table 1).
*   **Anatomical Staging:** Imaging (CT scan, May 2025) confirms a large, 10 cm mass in the pancreatic tail. This is a locally advanced tumor, consistent with a pT4 stage, showing extensive locoregional infiltration into the splenic hilum, gastric fundus, and left adrenal gland. There is also evidence of complete splenic vein occlusion by a tumor thrombus (ENETS 2023, Sec 3.1.3).
*   **Functional Imaging and Prognostic Markers:**
    *   The initial FDG-PET scan (Jan 2025) revealed a highly hypermetabolic mass (SUV 16). According to the ENETS 2023 and ESMO 2020 guidelines, high FDG uptake is a poor prognostic factor in NETs and is associated with higher tumor grade (ENETS 2023, Sec 2.5.1; ESMO 2020, Staging section).
    *   A significant limitation in the current workup is the absence of somatostatin receptor imaging (SRI), such as a 68Ga-DOTATATE PET/CT. The guidelines identify SRI as the functional imaging modality of choice for staging, prognostication, and determining eligibility for Peptide Receptor Radionuclide Therapy (PRRT) (ENETS 2023, Sec 2.5.2; ESMO 2020, Staging section). The tumor's SSTR expression status is therefore unknown.

### **Response to Prior Therapy**
*   The patient received 7 cycles of FOLFIRINOX from February to May 2025. While not a standard first-line therapy for Pan-NETs, this can be viewed as a form of neoadjuvant or conversion chemotherapy.
*   Imaging follow-up suggests disease stabilization, with a modest decrease in the primary mass size from up to 12 cm (April 2025) to 10 cm (May 2025). This indicates the tumor is not refractory to systemic chemotherapy.

### **Current Clinical Problem and Considerations for Management**
*   The patient's readmission is due to a new, severe upper GI bleed, which is a direct and life-threatening complication of the tumor's local invasiveness. This underscores the urgent need for definitive local control of the primary tumor.
*   **Surgical Candidacy:** The central question for the tumor board is the feasibility of surgical resection. The ENETS 2023 guidelines state that patients with locally advanced (T4) NF-Pan-NET can be resected safely in expert centers, and that radical (R0) resection, including venous reconstruction, can be considered (Level of evidence 3, Grade of recommendation A). The ESMO 2020 guidelines similarly support considering pancreatectomy with vascular resection in this setting. The patient's disease appears confined to the locoregional area without distant metastases on available imaging, making curative-intent surgery a primary consideration. The extent of infiltration will necessitate a complex multivisceral resection.
*   **Alternative Therapeutic Options:** If the tumor is deemed unresectable, or if the surgical risk is prohibitive, other systemic therapies would be considered. These include alternative chemotherapy regimens (e.g., temozolomide/capecitabine), targeted agents (everolimus, sunitinib), or PRRT (ESMO 2020, Figure 4). However, eligibility for PRRT cannot be assessed without SRI to confirm sufficient SSTR expression, and the high FDG uptake raises the possibility of SSTR-negative disease, which would be a contraindication.

### **Summary for Tumor Board Discussion**
This patient has a large, locally advanced, high-end G2 Pan-NET with aggressive biological features (high Ki-67, high FDG uptake) that is causing recurrent, life-threatening GI bleeding. He has demonstrated disease stabilization following systemic chemotherapy. The immediate clinical priority is to achieve local control to prevent further hemorrhage. The main therapeutic question is to determine if a complex, multivisceral surgical resection with curative intent is technically feasible and offers an acceptable risk-benefit profile, as supported by both ENETS and ESMO guidelines for locally advanced disease.
