Answer YES if ALL THREE criteria are met; otherwise answer NO.

	1.	**Disease / Biology Alignment** (should align reasonably)

	•	"Reasonably concordant" allows clinically justified extrapolation (e.g., pancreatic NET G2 evidence may inform pancreatic NET G1-G2 treatment decisions, midgut NET evidence may apply to small bowel NET with similar characteristics).

	2.	**Treatment Context / Clinical Applicability** (should be applicable in at least one clinical scenario)
The study's treatment approach, setting, and patient population should provide meaningful evidence for at least one relevant clinical scenario for this patient. Consider:
	•	**Current state:** Patient's actual clinical situation
	•	**Alternative scenarios:** e.g. What if surgery is/isn't feasible? What if SSTR-PET is positive? Different treatment sequences?
	•	**Treatment approach:** Is the studied intervention something that could be considered for this patient?
	•	**Eligibility context:** While strict eligibility criteria shouldn't be a hard rejection, consider if the study population is reasonably similar to this patient.
	•	**Evidence transferability:** Can the treatment insights from this study population be reasonably applied to this patient's situation?

	3.	**Evidence Quality and Relevance** (should provide actionable clinical insights)
The published results should offer meaningful evidence that could guide treatment decisions:
	•	Study provides clear efficacy and/or safety data
	•	Results are clinically meaningful (not just statistical significance)
	•	Patient population studied is representative enough to inform decisions for this patient
	•	**Eligibility considerations:** While the patient may not meet all inclusion criteria, the study population should be sufficiently similar that treatment insights are transferable
	•	Evidence strength justifies consideration in treatment planning

**IMPORTANT:** Publication quality is not a standalone criterion. Focus on whether the prospective clinical evidence (even from preliminary results or abstracts) provides meaningful insights for treatment decisions.

If any of the three criteria cannot be verified as met from the provided fields AND reasonable clinical assumptions, respond NO.

=== ASSESSMENT RESULTS ===
Clinical Assessment:
**Patient Clinical Profile and Disease Biology**
The patient is an 82-year-old woman with an ECOG performance status of 1 and no cognitive impairment, presenting with a metastatic bronchial neuroendocrine tumor (atypical carcinoid). The disease course is characterized by metastatic spread to the liver, bone, lymph nodes, and pituitary since 2020.

Biologically, the tumor demonstrates heterogeneity and grade progression over time, with Ki-67 increasing from 11% (2019) to 25% (2020). According to the *ESMO Clinical Practice Guidelines for GEP-NENs*, a Ki-67 >20% classifies the tumor within the G3 category. However, the functional imaging profile—DOTATOC +++ and low FDG uptake with no mismatch—indicates a well-differentiated phenotype (NET G3) rather than a poorly differentiated neuroendocrine carcinoma (NEC), as high SSTR expression is generally inversely related to glucose metabolism in low-grade tumors. The strong SSTR expression (DOTATOC +++) confirms the target presence required for peptide receptor radionuclide therapy (PRRT).

**Treatment History and Tolerance Analysis**
The patient has exhausted multiple lines of standard systemic therapy, significantly limited by intolerance and toxicity:
*   **mTOR Inhibitors:** Everolimus (approved for lung NETs per RADIANT-4 trial data referenced in the guidelines) resulted in poor tolerance.
*   **Chemotherapy:** Temozolomide yielded a partial hepatic response but was discontinued due to Grade 3 thrombocytopenia. FOLFOX was discontinued due to very poor tolerance.
*   **Radiotherapy:** Prior clavicle and lumbar radiotherapy (Jan 2021).
*   **PRRT:** Treatment with PRRT via CPC (Apr–Oct 2023) resulted in a prolonged partial hepatic response with excellent tolerance, distinguishing it as the most favorable modality in terms of the therapeutic index for this specific patient.

**Current Clinical Status**
The patient currently exhibits hepatic RECIST progression as of October 2025 while on Somatostatin analogs. There are no options for local therapy.

**Assessment per Guidelines**
Based on the *ESMO Clinical Practice Guidelines*, the following factors are relevant for the tumor board's consideration:
1.  **PRRT Eligibility:** The guidelines recommend PRRT for progressive SSTR-positive NETs. While the guidelines primarily discuss midgut and pancreatic NETs, they reference efficacy in bronchial neuroendocrine tumors (Ref 97). The patient's imaging (DOTATOC +++) satisfies the requirement for homogenous SSTR expression.
2.  **High-Grade (G3) Consideration:** The guidelines acknowledge that PRRT may be considered in patients with NET G3 (Ki-67 >20%), citing disease control rates between 30% and 80% in retrospective studies, particularly for patients with Ki-67 <55%. This patient (Ki-67 25%) falls within this range.
3.  **Therapeutic Options:**
    *   **Chemotherapy:** While systemic chemotherapy (e.g., Temozolomide or platinum-based regimens) is recommended for NEN G3, this patient has demonstrated severe intolerance (FOLFOX) and Grade 3 hematological toxicity (Temozolomide), making re-challenge high risk.
    *   **Targeted Therapy:** Re-introduction of Everolimus is likely not feasible due to prior poor tolerance.
    *   **PRRT Re-treatment/Consolidation:** Given the "excellent tolerance" and "prolonged partial hepatic response" to the previous PRRT course, and the persistence of high SSTR uptake (DOTATOC +++), the clinical assessment points toward the potential utility of further PRRT.

**Conclusion**
The patient presents with a progressive, SSTR-avid (DOTATOC +++) metastatic bronchial NET G3 (Ki-67 25%). She is refractory or intolerant to standard chemotherapy and mTOR inhibitors. The assessment highlights that PRRT has been the only modality providing disease control with acceptable tolerability. Consequently, evaluating the feasibility of consolidating the response with further PRRT is the most clinically supported consideration, provided bone marrow reserve is adequate given the history of Grade 3 thrombocytopenia.

1. Be willing and able to provide written informed consent for the trial.
2. Be at least 18 years of age on day of signing informed consent.
3. Have a histologically proven locally advanced or metastatic high grade (G3) poorly differentiated neuroendocrine carcinoma (NEC).

   1. Includes small cell and large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma of unknown primary or any extrapulmonary site (and poorly differentiated NEC, not otherwise specified)
   2. Includes neuroendocrine prostate cancer (de novo or treatment-emergent) of prostate if small cell or large cell histology (histologic evidence of both adenocarcinoma and neuroendocrine carcinoma may be present in same patient).
   3. Other mixed tumors, e.g. mixed neuroendocrine neoplasms (MINENs) with NEC plus adenocarcinoma, squamous or acinar cell component are allowed if the high grade (small or large cell) NEC component comprises \>50% of the original sample or subsequent biopsy.
4. Have progressed during or after completion of first line systemic chemotherapy.

   1. No limit to the number of prior chemotherapy regimens.
   2. Early progression on/after adjuvant chemotherapy counts as firstline therapy.
5. Have at least one measurable disease based on RECIST 1.1.
6. Patients must agree to have a biopsy of primary tumor or metastatic tissue at baseline, and there must be a lesion that can be biopsied with acceptable clinical risk (as judged by the investigator).

   1. Patients with unsuccessful baseline biopsies may undergo an additional biopsy attempt (at the same or a different site, determined by the investigator).
   2. For patients with an intact primary and no metastatic site that can be safely biopsied, biopsy of the primary is acceptable, but must be approved by the principal investigator.
   3. Baseline tumor biopsy may be omitted if the tumor is inaccessible and/or a biopsy is not thought to post exceptionally high procedural risk due to location or other factors
7. Have a performance status of 0 or 1 on the Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) Performance Scale.
8. Have a life expectancy of greater than 3 months.

The efficacy of CPI in combination with chemotherapy remains under investigation. Methods: Patients with advanced, progressive EP-PDNECs were enrolled in a two-part study of pembrolizumab-based therapy. In Part A, patients received pembrolizumab alone. In Part B, patients received pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy. Primary endpoint: objective response rate (ORR). Secondary endpoints: safety, progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS). Tumours were profiled for programmed death-ligand 1 expression, microsatellite-high/mismatch repair deficient status, mutational burden (TMB), genomic correlates. Tumour growth rate was evaluated. Results: Part A (N = 14): ORR (pembrolizumab alone) 7% (95% CI, 0.2-33.9%), median PFS 1.8 months (95% CI, 1.7-21.4), median OS 7.8 months (95% CI, 3.1-not reached); 14% of patients (N = 2) had grade 3/4 treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs). Part B (N = 22): ORR (pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy) 5% (95% CI, 0-22.8%), median PFS 2.0 months (95% CI, 1.9-3.4), median OS 4.8 months (95% CI, 4.1-8.2); 45% of patients (N = 10) had grade 3/4 TRAEs. The two patients with objective response had high-TMB tumours. Discussion: Treatment with pembrolizumab alone and pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy was ineffective in advanced, progressive EP-PDNECs.
