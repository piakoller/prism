**Treatment History and Response**
The patient received adjuvant/palliative chemotherapy consisting of six cycles of carboplatin and etoposide between February and June 2025. This regimen aligns with ENETS recommendations (Q6 and Q10), which establish platinum-etoposide as the standard first-line treatment for the aggressive NEC component of MiNEN. The patient achieved a metabolic and morphological partial response, indicating chemosensitivity. The primary clinical dilemma concerns the residual pararenal lesion. While imaging indicated a partial response, the biopsy performed in July 2025 revealed chronic inflammation with no evidence of neoplasia.

*   **Guideline Context:** According to ENETS 2023 (Q7), surgery for metastatic digestive NEC is generally not recommended due to poor prognosis and rapid disease progression. The patient's excellent performance status (ECOG 0) is a positive prognostic factor (ENETS Q3), allowing for potential future interventions should disease progression be detected.

**Conclusion**
The patient presents with a colonic MiNEN (small-cell NEC component, Ki-67 90%) in clinical and pathological remission regarding the residual pararenal lesion, based on the recent negative biopsy showing only chronic inflammation. There is currently no histological evidence of active disease to justify immediate local treatment (surgery or radiotherapy). The clinical assessment supports a strategy of close surveillance to monitor for potential recurrence or progression of the aggressive NEC component.
