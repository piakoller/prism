**1. Rationale for Systemic Chemotherapy (CAPTEM or STZ/5-FU):**
The patient presents with "bulky" (liver metastases up to 150 mm), symptomatic (abdominal pain), and biologically aggressive disease (FDG-positive, Ki-67 20%).
*   **Guideline Support:** ENETS (Q7) and ESMO guidelines recommend cytotoxic chemotherapy for patients with bulky, symptomatic, or significant tumor growth to achieve rapid tumor shrinkage (cytoreduction).
*   **Trial Evidence:**
    *   **NCT03217097 (MGMT-NET):** The provided results for this trial indicate that alkylating agents (like Temozolomide) are effective, particularly in tumors with MGMT deficiency. The trial reported a **Best Objective Response Rate (ORR) of 52.9%** in dMGMT tumors treated with alkylating agents, compared to 11.5% in pMGMT tumors. Even without MGMT status, alkylating agents remain a standard of care for pancreatic NETs.
    *   **NCT03728361:** Demonstrated that Nivolumab plus Temozolomide achieved an ORR of 32.1% in neuroendocrine neoplasms, supporting the activity of the Temozolomide backbone.
    *   **NCT03074513:** While Atezolizumab/Bevacizumab showed activity (ORR 20% in pNETs), this is lower than the potential response rates of cytotoxic chemotherapy (30-50% per guidelines and MGMT-NET data) required for this patient's bulky disease.

**2. Rationale for Contraindicating PRRT (177Lu-Dotatate):**
*   **Discordant Imaging:** The patient has **Ga68-negative** peritoneal carcinomatosis despite having Ga68-positive liver metastases. PRRT relies on target expression; lesions without uptake will not be treated.
*   **Trial Context:** While **NCT03972488 (NETTER-2)** established 177Lu-Dotatate as a first-line standard for G2/G3 NETs (PFS 22.8 months vs 8.5 months), eligibility required SSTR positivity in *all* target lesions. This patient does not meet the homogeneity criteria required for the high efficacy seen in NETTER-2.
*   **Guideline Support:** ENETS guidelines (Section 2.5.1) note that low uptake on SST-PET correlates with poor prognosis, and ESMO guidelines emphasize that PRRT requires homogenous SSTR expression.

This makes it a robust option for second-line therapy if the patient progresses on chemotherapy.
