=== ASSESSMENT RESULTS ===
Clinical Assessment:
**Patient Clinical Profile and Tumor Biology**
The patient is a 35-year-old female, recently postpartum, presenting with a metastatic, well-differentiated non-functioning pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor (NF-Pan-NET). The tumor is classified as G2 based on a Ki-67 index of 20%. This proliferation index is at the upper limit for G2 tumors (cutoffs of 3% and 20% for NET G2 and G3, respectively, per ENETS 2023). The patient has a significant tumor burden, characterized as "bulky," with a 75 mm primary pancreatic tail mass and multiple bilobar hepatic metastases measuring up to 150 mm.

**Functional Imaging and Heterogeneity**
The dual-tracer PET imaging (FDG and Ga68) reveals significant tumor heterogeneity, which is a critical factor for prognostic stratification and treatment planning:
1.  **FDG-Avidity:** The primary tumor, liver metastases, hepatic hilum adenopathy, peritoneal carcinomatosis, and thyroid nodules are FDG-positive. According to ENETS guidelines (Section 2.5.1), high FDG uptake correlates with a higher grade and is a strong poor prognostic factor.
2.  **Somatostatin Receptor (SSTR) Status:** While the primary tumor and liver metastases are Ga68-positive, the peritoneal carcinomatosis is notably **Ga68-negative** (FDG+ / Ga68–). Additionally, the right adrenal nodule shows a reverse pattern (FDG– / Ga68+).
3.  **PRRT Eligibility Assessment:** Per ESMO guidelines, PRRT requires homogenous SSTR expression where all NET lesions are positive. The presence of Ga68-negative peritoneal carcinomatosis indicates a lack of SSTR expression in this compartment. Consequently, these lesions would likely not respond to Peptide Receptor Radionuclide Therapy (PRRT), as eligibility requires uptake higher than liver background in all metastatic sites.

**Staging and Resectability**
The disease is advanced and metastatic. The primary tumor shows local invasion (splenic hilum, left adrenal gland, upper pole of left kidney). The presence of extensive bilobar liver metastases (up to 150 mm) and peritoneal carcinomatosis renders the patient unresectable for curative intent. Per ESMO guidelines, upfront surgery is generally excluded in the presence of extra-abdominal metastases (peritoneal involvement is extra-hepatic) or high-grade features, and debulking is controversial for non-functioning tumors with high Ki-67 (>10%).

**Genetic Considerations**
Given the patient's young age (35 years) and the diagnosis of Pan-NET, genetic counseling is indicated to rule out hereditary syndromes such as Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia type 1 (MEN1) or Von Hippel-Lindau (VHL) disease (ENETS 2.1). The presence of thyroid nodules (FDG+/Ga68+) warrants further investigation to exclude a synchronous malignancy or manifestations of a syndromic complex (e.g., MEN1).

**Therapeutic Assessment**
The clinical picture is dominated by bulky, symptomatic disease (abdominal pain for $\ge$1 year) with aggressive features (FDG positivity, Ki-67 20%).
*   **Systemic Therapy:** According to ENETS (Q7) and ESMO guidelines, chemotherapy is a suitable consideration for patients with bulky, symptomatic, or significant tumor growth, particularly when Ki-67 is high (G2/G3) or FDG uptake is present. Regimens such as Temozolomide/Capecitabine or Streptozotocin/5-FU are referenced for such scenarios.
*   **Somatostatin Analogues (SSAs):** While SSAs are the standard first-line for slow-growing, SSTR-positive G1/G2 tumors (ENETS Q6), this patient's high tumor burden, symptoms, and FDG positivity suggest a more aggressive biology that may require a more active antiproliferative approach than SSA monotherapy.
*   **Targeted Therapies:** Sunitinib and Everolimus are options for progressive disease but are generally cytostatic rather than cytotoxic (tumor shrinkage is less common compared to chemotherapy).

**Conclusion**
The patient presents with a high-risk, bulky, metastatic G2 Pan-NET with discordant imaging features (FDG-positive/Ga68-negative peritoneal disease). The imaging mismatch suggests dedifferentiation in the peritoneal compartment, limiting the utility of SSTR-targeted therapies (PRRT) for those specific lesions. The clinical priority is systemic control of a bulky, symptomatic, and biologically aggressive tumor.

	•	Summary: Neuroendocrine tumors (NET) are rare but their incidence is growing. Alkylating agents (ALKY) are one of the main systemic treatments used, at least for advanced duodeno-pancreatic NETs, with a response rate of 30 to 40% and a median progression-free survival of 4 to 18 months. Chemotherapy is one of the few therapeutic weapons, along with everolimus, somatostatin analogs, and metabolic radiotherapy, for lung NETs, called typical and atypical carcinoids, even if the level of proof of efficacy for these treatments is lower than for duodeno-pancreatic NETs. Considering the available retrospective data, O6-Methylguanine-DNA methyltransferase (MGMT) appears to be a predictive factor of the response to ALKY. Oxaliplatin (OX) has demonstrated an interesting activity, with response rates between 17% and 30%. In a first retrospective study we showed that Gemox is effective in NET, and more recently that its activity is similar to that of ALKYs, but without being influenced by the MGMT status. Prospective studies are needed but our data suggests that ALKY should be offered first to patients with methylated MGMT tumors while Oxaliplatin-based chemotherapy should be offered first to patients with unmethylated MGMT tumors.

In this project, we wish to evaluate the contribution of the MGMT methylation, evaluated in the tumor, in predicting the Objective Response (OR) in patients treated with ALKY and to evaluate a treatment with alkylating agents versus Oxaliplatin in patients with a duodeno-pancreatic or lung or unknown primitive NET.

PubMed Full Content: Full text links Cite Display options Display options Format Abstract PubMed PMID Abstract Purpose: Alkylating agents (ALKY) are the main chemotherapies used for advanced neuroendocrine tumors (NETs). O 6 -Methylguanine-DNA methyltransferase (MGMT) status, as proficient (p) or deficient (d), may predict the response to ALKY. Patients and methods: MGMT-NET (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT03217097 ) was a phase II trial randomly assigning 1:1 for pMGMT or 2:1 for dMGMT-NETs to either ALKY or oxaliplatin (Ox). Inclusion criteria were a confirmed advanced pancreatic, thoracic, or unknown primary NETs with an indication for chemotherapy and tissue available. The primary aim was to detect a difference of 35% between the 3-month objective response rate (ORR) in pMGMT-NETs versus in dMGMT-NETs when treated with ALKY. A biomarker-stratified design was performed to compare ALKY and Ox in the dMGMT and pMGMT strata for the secondary end points. dMGMT was defined using pyrosequencing (PSQ; methylated MGMT ≥9%) and using immunochemistry ( H -score of MGMT <50) when PSQ was not interpretable. Results: From October 2018 to October 2021, 105 patients (55 pancreas, 38 thorax, 12 unknown) started either ALKY (n = 62) or Ox (n = 43). The median age was 63 years (range, 30-84), and 59% were males. NETs were G1 (19%), G2 (69%), or G3 (10%). Among patients with interpretable MGMT status, 56.9% (58 of 102) had a dMGMT-NET. The primary end point was not reached; the 3-month ORR was 10 (29.4%) versus 2 (8%), and the odds ratio was 3.5 (0.58-21.16), P = .172. However, best ORR (18 [52.9%] v 3 [11.5%]) and median progression-free survival (14.6 [95% CI, 7.2 to 22.1] v 11.3 [9.4 to 13.2] months) were higher for dMGMT-NETs versus pMGMT-NETs. MGMT status does not seem to affect the Ox efficacy. Conclusion: Despite the fact that the primary end point was not reached, ALKY has clinical activity in patients with dMGMT-NETs.
