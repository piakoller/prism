**Clinical Justification for Cytotoxic Chemotherapy (CAPTEM)**
The patient presents with a metastatic, well-differentiated NF-Pan-NET G2 (Ki-67 20%) characterized by "bulky" disease (75 mm primary, 150 mm liver metastases), significant symptoms (abdominal pain), and high FDG avidity.
*   **Guideline Support:** According to **ENETS 2023 Guidelines (Q7)** and **ESMO Guidelines**, chemotherapy is the preferred first-line systemic therapy for Pan-NETs with bulky, symptomatic disease, or significant tumor growth, particularly when Ki-67 is high (G2/G3) or FDG uptake is present. The guidelines explicitly list Temozolomide/Capecitabine (CAPTEM) or Streptozotocin/5-FU as standard regimens for this profile.
*   **Trial Evidence for Alkylating Agents:** The **MGMT-NET trial (NCT03217097)** provides prospective evidence supporting the activity of alkylating agents (like Temozolomide) in Pan-NETs. In the trial's abstract, the alkylating agent arm demonstrated an Objective Response Rate (ORR) of **29.4%** in MGMT-deficient tumors (dMGMT) and **8%** in MGMT-proficient tumors, with a median Progression-Free Survival (PFS) of **14.6 months** in the dMGMT group. While MGMT testing is not always routine, the trial confirms the efficacy of this class of drugs. Additionally, **NCT03728361** (Nivolumab/Temozolomide) showed an ORR of **32.1%** in a mixed NET cohort, further supporting the cytotoxic backbone of Temozolomide.

**Contraindication for PRRT (177Lu-Dotatate)**
Despite the patient's tumor being G2 (Ki-67 20%), which would typically make her eligible for PRRT (supported by **NCT03972488/NETTER-2** for G2/G3 NETs), she is **ineligible** due to discordant imaging.
*   **Discordant Findings:** The patient has **Ga68-negative** peritoneal carcinomatosis (FDG+/Ga68-). PRRT relies on the binding of the radiopharmaceutical to somatostatin receptors. Lesions that are Ga68-negative lack sufficient receptor expression to target, meaning the peritoneal disease would likely progress unchecked on PRRT.
*   **Guideline Alignment:** ESMO and ENETS guidelines emphasize that PRRT requires homogenous SSTR expression. The presence of FDG-positive/Ga68-negative disease is a marker of dedifferentiation and poor prognosis, necessitating a cytotoxic approach (Chemotherapy) rather than a cytostatic or receptor-targeted one.

**Exclusion of Other Modalities**
*   **Somatostatin Analogues (SSAs):** While standard for indolent G1/G2 tumors (ENETS Q6), SSAs alone are insufficient for tumor shrinkage in bulky, symptomatic, FDG-positive disease.
*   **Immunotherapy Monotherapy:** **NCT03278379** (Avelumab) demonstrated an **ORR of 0%** in G2-3 NETs, supporting the exclusion of single-agent PD-L1 blockade for this patient.
*   **Targeted Therapies (Sunitinib/Everolimus):** While approved options (ENETS Q6), they are generally cytostatic with lower response rates (<10% ORR for Everolimus/Sunitinib in pivotal trials) compared to chemotherapy, making them less suitable for a patient requiring urgent debulking for symptom control.

This establishes Cabozantinib as a robust second-line option should the patient progress on chemotherapy.
