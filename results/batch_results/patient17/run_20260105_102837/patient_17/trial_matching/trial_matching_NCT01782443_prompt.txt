=== ASSESSMENT RESULTS ===
Clinical Assessment:
**Patient Summary and Clinical Status**
The patient is a 34-year-old male with a well-differentiated neuroendocrine tumor (NET) of the duodenum, initially diagnosed in 2016 (pT2 pN1, Ki-67 2-3%). The clinical course is characterized by recurrent metastatic spread to the liver despite multiple surgical interventions (pylorus-preserving partial duodeno-pancreatectomy in 2016, central liver resection in 2018, and resection of liver metastases in 2023). The patient has a good performance status (ECOG 0).

**Assessment of Disease Progression**
Current imaging (Gallium DOTATATE-PET/CT, August 2025) demonstrates disease progression with the appearance of two new liver metastases and suspicion of two lymph node metastases in the upper abdomen. This progression has occurred while the patient was under treatment with a Somatostatin analogue (SSA), which was continued after the 2023 surgery.

**Tumor Biology and Receptor Status**
The tumor was initially characterized as well-differentiated with a low proliferation index (Ki-67 2-3%). The detection of new metastases via Gallium DOTATATE-PET/CT confirms the expression of somatostatin receptors (SSTR) in the new lesions.

**Guideline-Based Clinical Assessment**

1.  **Systemic Therapy Escalation:**
    *   According to the **ESMO Clinical Practice Guidelines (2020)**, the goal of systemic therapy is to control tumor growth. Since the patient has shown radiological progression on SSA therapy, the guidelines suggest escalating treatment.
    *   **Peptide Receptor Radionuclide Therapy (PRRT):** Both **ENETS (2023)** and **ESMO (2020)** guidelines identify PRRT as a valid therapeutic option for progressive, SSTR-positive NETs. ESMO specifically notes that PRRT is recommended in patients with disease progression on SSAs. The patient's positive Gallium DOTATATE-PET/CT suggests potential eligibility. However, it must be clinically verified that the uptake in all tumor lesions (both liver and lymph nodes) exceeds the uptake of normal liver parenchyma (liver SUVmax is typically ~6–7) to confirm suitability for PRRT.
    *   **Targeted Molecular Therapy:** **ESMO (2020)** guidelines recommend Everolimus for patients with clearly progressive gastrointestinal NETs (based on the RADIANT-4 trial). **ENETS (2023)** also lists Everolimus as a therapeutic option for widespread metastatic gastroduodenal NETs, though it notes the evidence is limited compared to other sites.
    *   **Chemotherapy:** Systemic chemotherapy is generally not recommended for well-differentiated, slowly growing GI-NETs (Ki-67 <10%) according to **ESMO (2020)**, unless the tumor is rapidly progressive or the grade has increased.

2.  **Surgical Considerations:**
    *   **ENETS (2023)** guidelines state that surgery with curative intent for metastatic gastroduodenal NETs should be performed if a complete tumor resection (R0) seems feasible.
    *   **ESMO (2020)** recommends that a surgical approach for stage IV disease be considered in selected patients with exclusive or predominant liver involvement.
    *   Given the patient's history of two prior liver resections (2018, 2023) and the recurrence of disease within two years of the last surgery, along with new suspected extra-hepatic nodal involvement, the feasibility and benefit of a third liver resection must be weighed against the high likelihood of further systemic recurrence.

3.  **Re-evaluation of Tumor Grade:**
    *   While the initial Ki-67 was 2-3%, **ESMO (2020)** guidelines suggest that a re-biopsy may be considered in the follow-up of metastatic disease to exclude dedifferentiation (progression to higher grade), although the current slow progression over 9 years is consistent with a well-differentiated phenotype.

**Conclusion**
The patient presents with progressive, SSTR-positive metastatic disease while on maintenance SSA therapy. The clinical picture aligns with guideline criteria for escalating systemic therapy (switching to PRRT or Everolimus) or considering further surgical resection if technically feasible and oncologically sound.

	•	Summary: This research study is a Phase II clinical trial, which tests the safety and effectiveness of an investigational drug to learn whether the drug works in treating a specific cancer. "Investigational" means that the drug, Ziv-aflibercept, is being studied. It also means that the FDA has not yet approved Ziv-aflibercept for use in patients with your type of cancer.

Every person has molecules in their bloodstream called vascular endothelial growth factors (VEGFs). These molecules help grow and sustain new blood vessels needed by the human body. Cancer tumors hijack this mechanism because they need new blod vessels and oxygen to grow. Ziv-aflibercept is an antibody. Antibodies are proteins that are produced naturally in our bodies and help to recognize foreign substances in our body. Ziv-aflibercept is a "targeted therapy" called a "VEGF Trap", that "traps" (binds) these VEGFs and prevents the cancer from using them to grow.

Though Ziv-aflibercept has not yet been FDA approved for the treatment of carcinoid tumors, it has recently been approved for patients with treatment-resistant colorectal cancer.

In this research study, we will use Ziv-aflibercept in combination with standard octreotide therapy to see if it slows the growth or spread of your carcinoid tumor. Standard octreotide (sandostatin) therapy is currently approved for treating symptoms of carcinoid tumors, such as those caused by carcinoid syndrome. Carcinoid syndrome is caused by hormones and other substances released by carcinoid tumors into the bloodstream. One of these secreted substances is serotonin, one of the body's natural chemical messengers. When excess serotonin secreted by the carcinoid tumors reaches the body's tissues, it is thought to cause diarrhea and redness (flushing) of the face, chest or back. Excess serotonin may also cause changes in the structure of the heart valves, which can impair the heart's function. Octreotide works by binding to receptors found on carcinoid tumors and prevents the release of hormones from the tumor.

Methods: A single-arm, phase II trial enrolling patients with advanced, progressive extrapancreatic NET. Patients were treated with Ziv-aflibercept 4 mg/kg intravenously on day 1 and 15 of a 28-day cycle; the starting dose was reduced to 2 mg/kg on days 1 and 15 of a 28-day cycle because of hypertension-related events. The primary end point was progression-free survival. Results: The trial enrolled 19 patients (13 male:6 female). Patients received a median of 7 cycles (range, 1-18 cycles).
