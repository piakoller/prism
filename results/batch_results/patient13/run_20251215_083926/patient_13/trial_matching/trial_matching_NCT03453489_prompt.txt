Clinical Assessment:
**Patient Clinical Status and Tumor Biology**
The patient is a 53-year-old with a good performance status (ECOG 0–1) presenting with a metastatic, non-functioning pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor (NF-Pan-NET). The disease is sporadic (no MEN1) and characterized by a high hepatic tumor burden, with additional metastases in lymph nodes, bone, and lungs (low–moderate burden).

**Imaging Assessment**
Functional imaging reveals a "dual-positive" status:
1.  **DOTATOC-positive:** Indicates high expression of somatostatin receptors (SSTR), which is a prerequisite for Peptide Receptor Radionuclide Therapy (PRRT).
2.  **FDG-positive:** Indicates high metabolic activity, which correlates with the Grade 3 histology and generally predicts a more aggressive clinical course compared to FDG-negative disease.
3.  **No mismatch:** The absence of mismatch implies that the metabolically active (FDG-positive) lesions retain SSTR expression. According to ENETS and ESMO guidelines, SSTR-PET/CT is the first-line functional imaging method, and FDG-PET provides complementary prognostic information. High FDG uptake is a strong poor prognostic factor.

**Treatment History and Refractoriness**
The patient has demonstrated disease progression following multiple lines of systemic therapy, indicating a refractory disease state:
*   **Chemotherapy:** The patient achieved a partial response to FOLFOX but progressed after an initial response to 5-FU + dacarbazine. As dacarbazine is an alkylating agent similar to temozolomide, the patient has effectively progressed on both oxaliplatin-based and alkylating/antimetabolite regimens.
*   **Somatostatin Analogs (SSA):** Used for maintenance.
*   **Targeted Therapy:** The patient has progressed on Sunitinib (Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitor) as of July 2025.

**Guideline-Based Assessment for Next Steps**
Given the hepatic and overall progression in a Grade 3 NF-Pan-NET (Ki-67 25%), the following clinical considerations are derived from the ENETS 2023 and ESMO 2020 guidelines:

1.  **Peptide Receptor Radionuclide Therapy (PRRT):**
    *   **Eligibility:** The patient is DOTATOC-positive. ESMO guidelines state PRRT is approved for Pan-NETs and may be considered in NET G3 patients, though careful selection is required. ENETS notes promising results in G3 patients with Ki-67 < 55%.

The "no mismatch" finding supports eligibility, provided the intensity of uptake meets this threshold.
    *   **Clinical Context:** ENETS guidelines mention that PRRT can be considered as a second-line treatment or in selected G3 cases (often within clinical trials like NETTER-2 or COMPOSE). The high liver burden requires careful evaluation of liver function and toxicity risks.

2.  **Targeted Molecular Therapy (mTOR Inhibitors):**
    *   **Everolimus:** According to ENETS and ESMO guidelines, Everolimus is recommended in progressive G1 and G2 NF-Pan-NETs and could be considered in progressive G3 NF-Pan-NETs (Ki-67 < 55%). The patient has failed Sunitinib but has not yet received Everolimus. There is no cross-resistance defined between TKIs (Sunitinib) and mTOR inhibitors (Everolimus).

3.  **Chemotherapy Considerations:**

While ENETS mentions Temozolomide + Capecitabine for G3, the prior failure on a dacarbazine-containing regimen (an analog of temozolomide) suggests potential resistance to alkylating agents.

4.  **Locoregional Therapies:**
    *   Given the "high burden" of liver metastases, ESMO guidelines suggest locoregional therapies (e.g., embolization) could be considered in selected cases, potentially in combination with systemic therapy, though the presence of extrahepatic disease (bone, lungs) necessitates systemic control.

Based on the preserved SSTR expression (DOTATOC-positive) and failure of prior chemotherapy and Sunitinib, the primary guideline-based considerations for the tumor board involve evaluating the suitability for PRRT (confirming uptake intensity > liver background) or switching to a different targeted agent class such as Everolimus.
