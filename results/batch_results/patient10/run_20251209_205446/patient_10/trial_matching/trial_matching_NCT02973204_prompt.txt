The patient is a 70-year-old male with a heavily pre-treated, metastatic, non-functioning pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor (NF-Pan-NET). *   **PRRT (Peptide Receptor Radionuclide Therapy):** The patient received 4 cycles ending in March 2021. The best response was Stable Disease (SD). According to ENETS 2023 guidelines (Section Q5), retreatment with PRRT may be considered in patients who previously responded (including stabilization) and have a progression-free interval exceeding one year. The interval from March 2021 to the current progression exceeds this threshold.
*   **Targeted Therapies:** The patient demonstrated primary resistance to Everolimus (Best response: Progressive Disease) and Belzutifan (Best response: Progressive Disease). Sunitinib (7th line) was discontinued due to cumulative toxicity (asthenia, G2 thrombocytopenia) despite the patient recovering to fragility status after one month.
*   **Chemotherapy:** The patient achieved Stable Disease/minor response with Streptozocin-5FU (5th line) and Stable Disease with CAPOX (6th line). It is noted that while the patient received Capecitabine (within CAPOX) and Streptozocin (alkylating agent), the specific regimen of Temozolomide alone or in combination with Capecitabine (CAPTEM)—highlighted in ENETS Q7 and ESMO guidelines as a standard option—has not been explicitly documented in the history.

**3. Clinical Assessment of Late-Line Options and Limitations**
Based on the ENETS 2023 and ESMO 2020 guidelines, the following clinical factors are critical for assessing late-line options:

*   **PRRT Retreatment Assessment:**
    *   **Guideline Support:** ENETS guidelines (Section Q5) and ESMO guidelines support considering PRRT retreatment (Re-PRRT) in selected patients with prior response/stabilization and sufficient time since the last cycle.
    *   **Bone Marrow Safety:** The patient presents with persistent G1 thrombocytopenia (recovering from G2). ESMO guidelines warn that up to 3-4% of patients may develop irreversible bone marrow toxicity. The persistent thrombocytopenia requires careful evaluation of bone marrow reserve before considering Re-PRRT or cytotoxic chemotherapy.

*   **Targeted Therapy Assessment:**
    *   Re-challenge with TKIs or mTOR inhibitors is not supported by the guidelines given the prior progression on Everolimus and intolerance to Sunitinib.

The patient presents with progressive, liver-dominant metastatic disease after exhausting standard lines of therapy. The clinical assessment points toward two potential guideline-based avenues for evaluation, heavily constrained by the patient's fragility and hematological toxicity:
1.  **PRRT Retreatment:** Feasible based on the duration of response to the first course (>1 year since March 2021), contingent upon adequate bone marrow function and confirmed high uptake on updated SRI.

	•	Summary: Background Treatment and control of cancer is associated with high costs, to patients in the form of side effects and discomfort during investigations, to society in the form of expensive drugs and studies.

Circulating tumor cells (CTC) has received great attention as a cancer biomarker in trying to estimate future course in patients with breast cancer, colon cancer and prostate cancer. CTC is believed to be a crucial step in cancer spreading to the bloodstream and giving rise to metastases. Detection of circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) specifically adds specificity to the analysis of the CTC.

The investigators would like to with molecular biological methods predict which patients requires special monitoring and individualized therapy and explore these tests as clinical decision support.

Purpose and method

In a blood sample from patients with neuro-endocrine tumor (NET) and hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), the investigators will by cell separation, flow cytometry and DNA sequencing and digital polymerase chain reaction (PCR):

1. Identify and isolate the CTC and investigate these for tumor-specific mutations.
2. Quantify ctDNA and analyze this for specific mutations, which in the past has been found frequent in NET and HCC.
3. Compare findings of mutations on CTC and ctDNA with mutations in tissue biopsies.

The results are compared with the clinical data on disease course, including the effect of treatment and survival.

Subjects 40 Patients with small intestinal/unknown primary NET before treatment with somatostatin analogues 30 patients with pancreatic NET before treatment with Everolimus 30 patients with presumed radically treated HCC 30 patients with HCC in treatment with Sorafenib A blood sample will be taken prior to the start of treatment, after 1 month after start of treatment and thereafter every 3.-6. month for up to two years.

Perspectives In several cancer types molecular diagnostics have had significant influence in treatment and control strategy. The goal is in future to be able to take advantage of a so-called "liquid biopsy" as clinical decision support. The study will bring new knowledge to this growing field of research.
