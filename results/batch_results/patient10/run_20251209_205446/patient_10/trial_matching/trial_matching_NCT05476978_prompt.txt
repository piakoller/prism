The patient is a 70-year-old male with a heavily pre-treated, metastatic, non-functioning pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor (NF-Pan-NET). *   **PRRT (Peptide Receptor Radionuclide Therapy):** The patient received 4 cycles ending in March 2021. The best response was Stable Disease (SD). According to ENETS 2023 guidelines (Section Q5), retreatment with PRRT may be considered in patients who previously responded (including stabilization) and have a progression-free interval exceeding one year. The interval from March 2021 to the current progression exceeds this threshold.
*   **Targeted Therapies:** The patient demonstrated primary resistance to Everolimus (Best response: Progressive Disease) and Belzutifan (Best response: Progressive Disease). Sunitinib (7th line) was discontinued due to cumulative toxicity (asthenia, G2 thrombocytopenia) despite the patient recovering to fragility status after one month.
*   **Chemotherapy:** The patient achieved Stable Disease/minor response with Streptozocin-5FU (5th line) and Stable Disease with CAPOX (6th line). It is noted that while the patient received Capecitabine (within CAPOX) and Streptozocin (alkylating agent), the specific regimen of Temozolomide alone or in combination with Capecitabine (CAPTEM)—highlighted in ENETS Q7 and ESMO guidelines as a standard option—has not been explicitly documented in the history.

**3. Clinical Assessment of Late-Line Options and Limitations**
Based on the ENETS 2023 and ESMO 2020 guidelines, the following clinical factors are critical for assessing late-line options:

*   **PRRT Retreatment Assessment:**
    *   **Guideline Support:** ENETS guidelines (Section Q5) and ESMO guidelines support considering PRRT retreatment (Re-PRRT) in selected patients with prior response/stabilization and sufficient time since the last cycle.
    *   **Bone Marrow Safety:** The patient presents with persistent G1 thrombocytopenia (recovering from G2). ESMO guidelines warn that up to 3-4% of patients may develop irreversible bone marrow toxicity. The persistent thrombocytopenia requires careful evaluation of bone marrow reserve before considering Re-PRRT or cytotoxic chemotherapy.
    *   **Imaging Requirement:** Eligibility for PRRT retreatment strictly requires confirmation via Somatostatin Receptor Imaging (SRI), preferably PET/CT. As per standard nuclear medicine criteria, uptake in all metastatic liver lesions must exceed normal liver background activity. The current SRI status is not provided in the patient information.

*   **Chemotherapy Assessment:**
    *   **Guideline Support:** ENETS Q7 and ESMO guidelines identify Temozolomide-based regimens (e.g., CAPTEM) as active in Pan-NETs. While the patient has received alkylating agents (Streptozocin) and Capecitabine (in CAPOX), Temozolomide specifically has not been used.
    *   **Toxicity Barrier:** The cumulative toxicity from Sunitinib and persistent G1 thrombocytopenia significantly impact the risk-benefit ratio for further myelosuppressive chemotherapy (such as Temozolomide).

*   **Targeted Therapy Assessment:**
    *   Re-challenge with TKIs or mTOR inhibitors is not supported by the guidelines given the prior progression on Everolimus and intolerance to Sunitinib.

**4. Conclusion**
The patient presents with progressive, liver-dominant metastatic disease after exhausting standard lines of therapy. The clinical assessment points toward two potential guideline-based avenues for evaluation, heavily constrained by the patient's fragility and hematological toxicity:
1.  **PRRT Retreatment:** Feasible based on the duration of response to the first course (>1 year since March 2021), contingent upon adequate bone marrow function and confirmed high uptake on updated SRI.
2.  **Temozolomide-based chemotherapy:** A potential systemic option per ENETS Q7, though the patient's persistent thrombocytopenia and fragility may limit tolerability.

PubMed Full Content: Full text links Cite Display options Display options Format Abstract PubMed PMID Abstract Importance: Diagnosing solid lesions in the pancreas via endoscopic ultrasonographic (EUS) images is challenging. Artificial intelligence (AI) has the potential to help with such diagnosis, but existing AI models focus solely on a single modality. Objective: To advance the clinical diagnosis of solid lesions in the pancreas through developing a multimodal AI model integrating both clinical information and EUS images. Design, setting, and participants: In this randomized crossover trial conducted from January 1 to June 30, 2023, from 4 centers across China, 12 endoscopists of varying levels of expertise were randomly assigned to diagnose solid lesions in the pancreas with or without AI assistance. Endoscopic ultrasonographic images and clinical information of 439 patients from 1 institution who had solid lesions in the pancreas between January 1, 2014, and December 31, 2022, were collected to train and validate the joint-AI model, while 189 patients from 3 external institutions were used to evaluate the robustness and generalizability of the model. Intervention: Conventional or AI-assisted diagnosis of solid lesions in the pancreas. Main outcomes and measures: In the retrospective dataset, the performance of the joint-AI model was evaluated internally and externally. In the prospective dataset, diagnostic performance of the endoscopists with or without the AI assistance was compared. Results: The retrospective dataset included 628 patients (400 men [63.7%]; mean [SD] age, 57.7 [27.4] years) who underwent EUS procedures. A total of 130 patients (81 men [62.3%]; mean [SD] age, 58.4 [11.7] years) were prospectively recruited for the crossover trial. The area under the curve of the joint-AI model ranged from 0.996 (95% CI, 0.993-0.998) in the internal test dataset to 0.955 (95% CI, 0.940-0.968), 0.924 (95% CI, 0.888-0.955), and 0.976 (95% CI, 0.942-0.995) in the 3 external test datasets, respectively. The diagnostic accuracy of novice endoscopists was significantly enhanced with AI assistance (0.69 [95% CI, 0.61-0.76] vs 0.90 [95% CI, 0.83-0.94]; P < .001), and the supplementary interpretability information alleviated the skepticism of the experienced endoscopists. Conclusions and relevance: In this randomized crossover trial of diagnosing solid lesions in the pancreas with or without AI assistance, the joint-AI model demonstrated positive human-AI interaction, which suggested its potential to facilitate a clinical diagnosis. Nevertheless, future randomized clinical trials are warranted. Trial registration: ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT05476978 .
