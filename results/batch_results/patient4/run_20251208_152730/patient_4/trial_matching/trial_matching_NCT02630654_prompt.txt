Clinical Assessment:
**Patient Clinical Profile and Disease Status**
The patient is a 68-year-old female with a well-differentiated, Grade 2 (Ki-67 3–5%) functional duodenal neuroendocrine tumor (NET), initially diagnosed in 2013. The disease course is characterized by synchronous liver metastases and a long natural history (12 years). The patient presents with liver-only disease progression (confirmed by RECIST PD in October 2025) following a sequence of extensive multimodal treatments.

**Treatment History and Resistance Profile**
The patient is heavily pre-treated and has demonstrated disease progression on multiple therapeutic lines:
1.  **Somatostatin Analogues (SSA):** Progressed after initial control (started 2013).
2.  **Locoregional and Surgical Therapy:** Underwent TAE, resection of the primary duodenal neoplasm, and liver resection (2013–2014), followed by multiple subsequent locoregional therapies.
3.  **Peptide Receptor Radionuclide Therapy (PRRT):** Administered in 2017–2018 due to liver progression.
4.  **Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitors (TKIs):**
    *   **Surufatinib:** Achieved long-term control (>40 cycles) within a phase II trial but eventually progressed.
    *   **Sunitinib:** Initiated in June 2025 for mild hepatic progression; resulted in stable disease initially but failed by October 2025 (RECIST PD).

**Current Clinical Assessment**
Despite radiological progression, the patient maintains an excellent performance status (ECOG PS 0) and is currently asymptomatic ("no relevant clinical symptoms"), suggesting a dissociation between tumor growth and clinical deterioration. The tumor remains well-differentiated with a relatively low proliferative index (Ki-67 3–5%).

**Guideline-Based Assessment of Management Options**
Based on the *ESMO Clinical Practice Guidelines for diagnosis, treatment and follow-up*, the following factors are relevant for the tumor board's assessment:

*   **Systemic Therapy (mTOR Inhibitors):** The patient has progressed on TKIs (Surufatinib and Sunitinib). The guidelines recommend Everolimus for the treatment of advanced, non-functional neuroendocrine tumors of the gastrointestinal tract (RADIANT-4 trial). While the patient's tumor was originally functional, she is currently asymptomatic. The guidelines note that Everolimus should be used in patients with clearly progressive GI NETs.
*   **Chemotherapy:** The guidelines state that results with systemic chemotherapy for advanced well-differentiated G1/G2 GI NETs are poor (ORR 11.5%) and generally "cannot be recommended." Chemotherapy is typically reserved for rapidly progressive tumors or those with higher Ki-67 (e.g., 15–20%). Given this patient's Ki-67 of 3–5% and long disease history, chemotherapy may have limited utility.
*   **Locoregional Therapy:** As the patient presents with liver-only progression, the guidelines suggest that vascular and ablative locoregional modalities (e.g., embolization, ablation) can be considered as an alternative or adjunct to systemic therapy in patients with liver-limited disease.
*   **PRRT Re-treatment Consideration:** The patient received PRRT 7–8 years ago. While the guidelines primarily discuss PRRT as a second-line therapy after SSA failure, they acknowledge it as a therapeutic option in progressive SSTR-positive NETs. Re-evaluation of Somatostatin Receptor (SSTR) status is essential.
    *   *Crucial Constraint:* Eligibility for any further PRRT consideration requires confirmation via [68]Ga/[64]Cu-SSTR-PET-CT that uptake in all metastatic lesions is significantly higher than the liver background. If lesions show low or heterogeneous uptake, PRRT is generally not indicated.
*   **Surufatinib Context:** The guidelines acknowledge recent data (SANET-ep) indicating activity of Surufatinib in extra-pancreatic NETs, consistent with the patient's prior response, though she has now developed resistance to this class.

**Diagnostic Requirements**
To finalize the management plan, updated staging and molecular imaging are necessary to characterize the metabolic activity and receptor status of the progressing hepatic lesions, specifically to distinguish between slow progression suitable for locoregional control versus systemic progression requiring a switch in drug class (e.g., to an mTOR inhibitor).

PubMed Full Content: Full text links Cite Display options Display options Format Abstract PubMed PMID Abstract Background: Small intestinal neuroendocrine tumors (SI-NETs) are difficult to diagnose in the early stage of disease. Current blood biomarkers such as chromogranin A (CgA) and 5-hydroxyindolacetic acid have low sensitivity (SEN) and specificity (SPE). This is a first preplanned interim analysis (Nordic non-interventional, prospective, exploratory, EXPLAIN study [ NCT02630654 ]). Its objective is to investigate if a plasma protein multi-biomarker strategy can improve diagnostic accuracy (ACC) in SI-NETs. Methods: At the time of diagnosis, before any disease-specific treatment was initiated, blood was collected from patients with advanced SI-NETs and 92 putative cancer-related plasma proteins from 135 patients were analyzed and compared with the results of age- and sex-matched controls (n = 143), using multiplex proximity extension assay and machine learning techniques. Results: Using a random forest model including 12 top ranked plasma proteins in patients with SI-NETs, the multi-biomarker strategy showed SEN and SPE of 89 and 91%, respectively, with negative predictive value (NPV) and positive predictive value (PPV) of 90 and 91%, respectively, to identify patients with regional or metastatic disease with an area under the receiver operator characteristic curve (AUROC) of 99%. In 30 patients with normal CgA concentrations, the model provided a diagnostic SPE of 98%, SEN of 56%, and NPV 90%, PPV of 90%, and AUROC 97%, regardless of proton pump inhibitor intake. Conclusion: This interim analysis demonstrates that a multi-biomarker/machine learning strategy improves diagnostic ACC of patients with SI-NET at the time of diagnosis, especially in patients with normal CgA levels. The results indicate that this multi-biomarker strategy can be useful for early detection of SI-NETs at presentation and conceivably detect recurrence after radical primary resection.
