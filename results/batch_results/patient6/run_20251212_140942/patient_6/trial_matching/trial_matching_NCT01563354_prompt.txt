=== ASSESSMENT RESULTS ===
Clinical Assessment:
**Patient Clinical Summary**
The patient is a 51-year-old female with a metastatic well-differentiated pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor (Pan-NET), Grade 2, with a Ki-67 index of 15%. She has a history of synchronous bilobar liver metastases diagnosed in 2018. The primary tumor was resected (total gastrectomy, splenectomy, distal pancreatectomy) in September 2018. She has been heavily pre-treated with somatostatin analogs (lanreotide), tyrosine kinase inhibitors (sunitinib), and liver-directed therapies (TAE). Most recently, she achieved stable disease (SD) for >2 years on the HIF-2α inhibitor belzutifan (40 mg/day) within a clinical trial but is now experiencing mild hepatic progression.

**Assessment of Liver Transplantation Feasibility**
Based on the **ESMO Clinical Practice Guidelines (2020)**, liver transplantation (LT) is a valid option only in *very selected* patients. The assessment of this patient against the specific ESMO criteria for LT is as follows:

*   **Criteria Met:**
    *   **Age:** The patient is 51 years old, meeting the criterion of age <60 years.
    *   **Primary Tumor Resection:** The primary tumor was removed in 2018.
    *   **Extrahepatic Disease:** Current evaluation (including exploratory laparoscopy) indicates liver-only disease.

*   **Criteria Not Met / Areas of Concern:**
    *   **Tumor Grade/Proliferation:** The patient's Ki-67 index is 15%. The ESMO guidelines explicitly state that LT criteria include histological confirmation of a well-differentiated NET with **Ki-67 <10%**. This patient falls outside this strict selection criterion.
    *   **Disease Stability:** The guidelines require stable disease in response to therapy for at least 6 months *before* transplant consideration. While the patient had long-term stability on belzutifan, she is currently presenting with "mild hepatic progression," which conflicts with the requirement for pre-transplant stability.
    *   **Tumor Burden:** The guidelines require metastatic diffusion <50% of the total liver volume. The specific liver tumor burden percentage is not provided in the patient information and requires calculation to determine eligibility.

**Diagnostic Assessment and Discrepancies**
*   **Bone Pain vs. Liver-Only Status:** The patient reports "mild, migratory bone pain," yet is classified as having "liver-only disease." The ESMO guidelines note that CT sensitivity for bone metastases is poor (61%, range 46%–80%). Since the patient has been followed up with CT scans, occult bone metastases cannot be ruled out based on the provided history.
*   **Imaging Recommendations:** To confirm the "absence of extrahepatic disease" required for LT consideration, and to investigate the reported bone pain, **SST-PET/CT** (or MRI for bone/liver) is indicated. ENETS 2023 guidelines recommend SST-PET/CT for whole-body staging and note it is superior to CT for skeletal lesions.

**Assessment of Current Therapy and Alternatives**
*   **Belzutifan:** The patient is progressing on belzutifan. The ENETS 2023 guidelines note that while belzutifan is approved for VHL-associated Pan-NETs, its value in metastatic Pan-NET outside of VHL is currently "unclear."
*   **PRRT Assessment:** If LT is deemed unfeasible due to the Ki-67 index (15%) or progression, Peptide Receptor Radionuclide Therapy (PRRT) is a potential consideration for progressive disease. However, eligibility requires confirmation of sufficient uptake on SST-imaging. It must be acknowledged that PRRT eligibility requires DOTATATE/DOTATOC PET uptake higher than liver background in all primary tumor lesions and metastases. If lesions show heterogeneous or low uptake, PRRT is generally not indicated.
*   **Chemotherapy:** According to ESMO guidelines, systemic chemotherapy (e.g., streptozotocin/5-FU or temozolomide/capecitabine) is recommended in progressive G2 Pan-NETs, particularly when there is bulky disease or progression on other therapies.

**Conclusion**
While the patient meets the age and primary resection criteria for liver transplantation, she does not meet the strict ESMO guideline criteria regarding Ki-67 index (<10% required; patient is 15%) and current disease stability (patient is progressing). Furthermore, the reported bone pain necessitates urgent exclusion of extrahepatic bone metastases via high-sensitivity imaging (SST-PET/CT) before any definitive decision on "liver-only" procedures can be made.

PubMed Full Content: Full text links Cite Display options Display options Format Abstract PubMed PMID Abstract Background: There are no data from prospective studies focused exclusively on patients with advanced lung and thymic carcinoids. We aimed to assess the efficacy and safety of long-acting pasireotide and everolimus, administered alone or in combination, in patients with advanced carcinoids of the lung or thymus. Methods: LUNA was a prospective, multicentre, randomised, open-label, phase 2 trial of adult patients (aged >18 years) with advanced (unresectable or metastatic), well differentiated carcinoid tumours of the lung or thymus, with radiological progression within 12 months before randomisation, and a WHO performance status of 0-2. At each centre, the investigator or their designee registered each patient using an interactive voice recognition system into one of the three treatment groups. The randomisation allocation sequence was generated by an external company; patients were randomly assigned (1:1:1) to receive treatment with long-acting pasireotide (60 mg intramuscularly every 28 days), everolimus (10 mg orally once daily), or both in combination, for the core 12-month treatment period. Patients were stratified by carcinoid type (typical vs atypical) and line of study treatment (first line vs others). The primary endpoint was the proportion of patients progression-free at month 9, defined as the proportion of patients with overall lesion assessment at month 9 showing a complete response, partial response, or stable disease according to local Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors, version 1.1, assessed in the intention-to-treat population. Safety was assessed in all patients who received at least one dose of study drug and had at least one post-baseline safety assessment. The trial is registered with ClinicalTrials.gov, number NCT01563354 . The extension phase of the study is ongoing. Findings: Between Aug 16, 2013, and Sept 30, 2014, 124 patients were enrolled from 36 centres in nine countries: 41 were allocated to the long-acting pasireotide group, 42 to the everolimus group, and 41 to the combination group. At month 9, the proportion of patients with an overall lesion assessment of complete response, partial response, or stable disease was 16 of 41 patients (39·0%, 95% CI 24·2-55·5) in the long-acting pasireotide group, 14 of 42 patients (33·3%, 19·6-49·5) in the everolimus group, and 24 of 41 patients (58·5%, 42·1-73·7) in the combination group. The most common grade 1-2 adverse events with a suspected association with long-acting pasireotide monotherapy were diarrhoea (15 [37%] of 41), hyperglycaemia (17 [41%]), and weight loss (8 [20%]); those with a suspected association with everolimus monotherapy were stomatitis (26 [62%] of 42) and diarrhoea (16 [38%]); and those suspected to be associated with combination treatment were hyperglycaemia (27 [66%] of 41]), diarrhoea (19 [46%]), and asthenia (8 [20%]). The most common grade 3-4 adverse events with a suspected association with long-acting pasireotide monotherapy were γ-glutamyltransferase increased (four [10%] of 41 patients), diarrhoea (three [7%]), and hyperglycaemia (three [7%]); those for everolimus were hyperglycaemia (seven [17%] of 42 patients), stomatitis (four [10%]), and diarrhoea (three [7%]); those for combination treatment were hyperglycaemia (nine [22%] of 41 patients) and diarrhoea (four [10%]). 11 patients died during the core 12-month treatment phase or up to 56 days after the last study treatment exposure date: two (5%) of 41 in the long-acting pasireotide group, six (14%) of 42 in the everolimus group, and three (7%) of 41 in the combination group. No deaths were suspected to be related to long-acting pasireotide treatment. One death in the everolimus group (acute kidney injury associated with diarrhoea), and two deaths in the combination group (diarrhoea and urinary sepsis in one patient, and acute renal failure and respiratory failure in one patient) were suspected to be related to everolimus treatment. In the latter patient, acute renal failure was not suspected to be related to everolimus treatment, but respiratory failure was suspected to be related. Interpretation: The study met the primary endpoint in all three treatment groups. Safety profiles were consistent with the known safety profiles of these agents. Further studies are needed to confirm the antitumour efficacy of the combination of a somatostatin analogue with everolimus in lung and thymic carcinoids. Funding: Novartis Pharma AG.
